Treewidth

Find a tree decomposition with a small largest bag.

The research question

The treewidth of a graph measures how far it is from being a tree: a tree decomposition arranges subsets of the vertices ("bags") into a tree so that (i) every vertex of G appears in at least one bag, (ii) every edge of G lies inside at least one bag, and (iii) the bags containing any vertex v form a connected subtree. The width of a decomposition is its largest bag size minus one; the treewidth of G is the minimum width over all decompositions. Deciding treewidth is NP-complete, and good decompositions are the workhorse of parameterized algorithms, probabilistic inference, and compiler register allocation.
