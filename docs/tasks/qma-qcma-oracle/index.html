The research question

The theorem comes from quantum complexity. QMA is the quantum analogue of NP: a polynomial-time quantum verifier checks a quantum witness state; QCMA is the variant where the witness must be a classical bit string. The theorem is a classical oracle separation between the two, stated through a decision problem called spectral Forrelation: the oracle describes two subsets S, U of the boolean hypercube, and the task is to decide whether there is a quantum state whose standard-basis measurement distribution is well supported on S while its Fourier-basis distribution is well supported on U — equivalently, whether ‖Π_U · H^{⊗n} · Π_S‖²_op is large (yes instance, ≥ 59/100) or small (no instance, ≤ 57/100). The theorem states: beyond a size threshold depending only on the resource bound a·n^a, spectral Forrelation at gap 59/100 vs 57/100 has a constant-query QMA verifier, while every classical-witness query algorithm within the bound misclassifies some instance. Three proved facts are supplied as hypotheses: the QMA amplification theorem, the core-matrix concentration, and the bosonic compressed-oracle sampling upper bound.
