Course timetabling

Schedule university lectures in rooms and periods while avoiding conflicts and reducing soft penalties.

Task results ↓

Mathematical setting
lecture⟼(room,period)\text{lecture}\longmapsto(\text{room},\text{period})lecture⟼(room,period)

The research question

The curriculum-based course timetabling problem (CB-CTT): a university publishes curricula (sets of courses that students of a programme take together), and every course has a teacher, a number of weekly lectures, a minimum number of days those lectures should spread over, and a student count. Assign every lecture to a room and a period (day × timeslot) of the week.

A timetable is feasible only if it violates none of the four hard constraints:

  • Lectures: every lecture of every course is scheduled (no missing, no extra lectures);
  • Conflicts: no two lectures share a period if they have the same teacher or belong to the same curriculum;
  • Availability: no lecture sits in a period declared unavailable for its course;
  • RoomOccupation: no two lectures share a room in the same period.

Among feasible timetables, quality is the soft-constraint penalty (lower is better):

  • RoomCapacity: for each lecture, 1 point per student above the room's seat count;
  • MinimumWorkingDays: 5 points per day below the course's minimum spread;
  • CurriculumCompactness: 2 points per lecture of a curriculum that is isolated (no adjacent lecture of the same curriculum on the same day);
  • RoomStability: 1 point per additional distinct room used by a course.

CB-CTT is NP-hard. The instances are real university data.
