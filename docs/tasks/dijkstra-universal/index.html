Universal optimality of Dijkstra

Prove the stated time and comparison bounds for Dijkstra’s algorithm with a working-set heap.

Task results ↓

Mathematical setting

The research question

The theorem comes from algorithm analysis. A distance order of a directed graph with a source s is a total order of the vertices in which every vertex other than s has an incoming forward arc (an arc from an earlier vertex); write D for the number of distance orders and F for the maximum number of forward arcs of any distance order.

The theorem states: there is a constant C > 0 such that on every directed graph with n > 2 vertices (all reachable from the source), Dijkstra traces exist on every non-negative arc-length assignment; every Dijkstra trace has time at most C·(m + log₂ D) and at most C·(F + log₂ D) comparisons, where the costs are those of a heap with the working-set bound; every correct time-model algorithm (decision trees with incidence-list access) spends at least (m + log₂ D)/C time on some arc lengths and representation; and every correct comparison-model algorithm (linear-function decision trees) makes at least (F − n + 1 + log₂ D)/C comparisons on some arc lengths.

In other words, Dijkstra's algorithm with a working-set-bound heap is universally optimal in time, and universally optimal in comparisons up to an additive term in n. An interval-DAG bound is given as the hypothesis hidag.
