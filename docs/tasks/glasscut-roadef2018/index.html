Glass cutting

Produce ordered rectangular pieces with valid guillotine cuts while minimizing wasted glass.

Task results ↓

Mathematical setting
min⁡ used area−piece area−reusable residual\min\ \text{used area}-\text{piece area}-\text{reusable residual}min used area−piece area−reusable residual

The research question

Flat glass plates are cut into ordered rectangular pieces. Plates are 6000 × 3210 mm and arrive in a fixed order; each plate carries defects (small rectangles that no produced piece may overlap). Pieces are grouped in stacks, and the pieces of each stack must come off the line in the given sequence (rebuilt from the cutting patterns by a depth-first traversal). Cuts are guillotine cuts: a piece must be obtainable with at most 3 cuts, plus at most one extra "trimming" cut inside each sub-plate left after a 3-cut (a branch at cut depth 3 or 4 has at most 2 children). First cuts are always vertical, with widths between 100 and 3500 mm (wastes excepted); second-cut heights are at least 100 mm; every waste rectangle is at least 20 × 20 mm. Pieces may be rotated 90°.

The objective is to minimize glass waste: with m used plates of size W × H, item areas wᵢhᵢ, and the reusable residual of width r on the last used plate (the full-height strip right of the last 1-cut),

waste = m·W·H − H·r − Σᵢ wᵢ·hᵢ          (minimize)
