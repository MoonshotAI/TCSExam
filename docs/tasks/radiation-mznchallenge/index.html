Radiation treatment planning

Match a prescribed intensity matrix exactly, attain its minimum beam-on time, and minimize the number of aperture shapes.

The research question

In intensity-modulated radiation therapy (IMRT) planning, an m × n grid specifies the nonnegative integer dose I[i,j] required at every cell. A multileaf collimator delivers a shape: a binary matrix whose exposed cells form at most one contiguous interval in each row. Different rows may use different intervals, and an empty row is allowed.

Delivering shape S_j for a positive integer time b_j adds b_j to every exposed cell. All shapes must have the same dimensions as I, and their weighted sum must equal I cell by cell.

This task first requires the total beam-on time to attain the structural lower bound

L(I) = max_i (I[i,1] + sum_{j=2}^n max(I[i,j] - I[i,j-1], 0)).

The judged instances admit a decomposition attaining this bound. A decomposition with total beam-on time greater than L(I) scores zero, even when it matches the dose exactly. Among decompositions with total beam-on time L(I), the objective is to minimize the number of shapes.
