Height-limited rooted forests

Choose a rooted forest inside the given graph to maximize the depth-weighted vertex score.

The research question

You are given a planar graph: N = 1000 vertices, placed uniformly at random inside a circle, connected by the Delaunay triangulation of those points (1000 <= M <= 3000 edges). Every vertex v has a positive integer weight A[v] (between 1 and 100). The height limit is H = 10.

You must split the vertices into rooted trees: for every vertex you either declare it a root, or choose one parent, such that

  • the parent edge (v, parent[v]) exists in the given graph,
  • following parent pointers never loops (every chain ends at a root), so each vertex gets a well-defined depth h[v] (roots have h = 0),
  • every depth is at most H: h[v] <= 10.

Each vertex may appear in exactly one tree, which the parent-pointer structure guarantees automatically. Your raw score for an instance is

raw = 1 + sum_v (h[v] + 1) * A[v]
