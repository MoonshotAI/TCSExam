Pseudorandomness of Forrelation

Prove the specified pseudorandomness bound for Forrelation against Boolean distinguishers with bounded Fourier mass.

The research question

The theorem comes from pseudorandomness. The Forrelation distribution F_n is a distribution over pairs of Boolean functions f, g : {±1}ⁿ → {±1}: first f is sampled uniformly at random, and then, independently for each input x, the value g(x) is ±1 with bias trnc(√(ε2ⁿ)·f̂(x)), where ε = 1/(100n), f̂ is the Fourier coefficient of f, and trnc truncates to [−1,1]. A well-known consequence of the theorem is pseudorandomness against AC⁰[2^poly(n), O(1)] circuits; the statement packaged here is the more general bound: for N = 2ⁿ and any Boolean C on 2N variables all of whose ±1-restrictions have level-2 Fourier mass L_{1,2} = ∑_{|S|=2} |Ĉ(S)| at most t,

|E_{(f,g)∼F_n}[C(tt f, tt g)] − E_{z uniform}[C(z)]| ≤ O(max(t,1)·log N/√N).

Two external facts are packaged as hypotheses (deep or textbook results; your proof may assume them): a Gaussian-fooling theorem for bounded level-2 Fourier mass (gaussianFoolingProp) and explicit Gaussian tail / truncated-second-moment bounds (gaussianTailProp).

The single theorem you must prove:

forrelation_pseudorandom — there exists K > 0 such that for every n ≥ 1, every multilinear C on the 2N variables that is ±1-valued on the cube, and every t ≥ 0 bounding the level-2 Fourier mass of all ±1-restrictions of C, the displayed bound holds with constant K.
