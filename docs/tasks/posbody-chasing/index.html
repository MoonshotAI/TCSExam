Chasing positive bodies

Prove the logarithmic competitive guarantee for the specified memoryless positive-body chasing algorithm.

The research question

The theorem comes from online algorithms. In positive body chasing, an online algorithm sees a sequence of constraints in the nonnegative orthant and must maintain a feasible point while paying for weighted increases in its coordinates. Each constraint is a single halfspace — a covering constraint ⟨c^t, x⟩ ≥ 1 or a packing constraint ⟨p^t, x⟩ ≤ 1 + ε — and cost is measured by the upward recourse ∑_t ∑_i w_i·(x^t_i − x^{t-1}_i)⁺. The algorithm answers each violated constraint with a single Kullback–Leibler divergence projection (a covering update with an additive offset ε/(4·d^t·c^t_i), where d^t is the row sparsity); it is presented here only through a response bundle ChaseRun of the properties the analysis uses (the trajectory and the Karush–Kuhn–Tucker multipliers, with tightness and multiplier conditions, monotonicity, and frozenness outside the support). The theorem states: the algorithm's upward recourse is at most K = (2 + ε)(1 + ε)·log(1 + 40d²/ε²)/ε times the upward recourse of every exactly-feasible benchmark trajectory.
