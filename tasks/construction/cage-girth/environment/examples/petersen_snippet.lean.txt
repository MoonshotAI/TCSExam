/- Worked example for cage-girth-lean-open: the Petersen graph as a
CageGraph witness. Petersen is the (3,5)-cage (n(3,5) = 10), so it only
counts on the closed sanity cell (3,5) — it does not count — but proving this
claim exercises the whole pipeline: fixed layer, graph representation,
native_decide. Copy the definitions between the fixed part and the
claim of /app/Task/Basic.lean, and the proof term into the claim body.

Petersen as the Kneser graph K(5,2): vertices = the ten 2-subsets of
{0,..,4} (encoded as 5-bit masks), adjacent iff disjoint. Every vertex has
exactly 3 disjoint 2-subsets; the girth is 5. -/

def petersen : CageGraph where
  n := 10
  adj := fun u v =>
    let mu := #[1+2, 1+4, 1+8, 1+16, 2+4, 2+8, 2+16, 4+8, 4+16, 8+16][u]!
    let mv := #[1+2, 1+4, 1+8, 1+16, 2+4, 2+8, 2+16, 4+8, 4+16, 8+16][v]!
    mu &&& mv == 0

theorem petersen_wf : WellFormed petersen := by native_decide
theorem petersen_reg : IsKRegular petersen 3 := by native_decide
theorem petersen_girth : GirthGE petersen 5 := by native_decide

-- (the sanity cell: valid proof, does not count).

graph of the affine group AGL(1,17) of order 272 = 17*16 (Biggs 1989), and
the 2026 records are lifts over small groups (Exoo et al. 2026). At the
