# Independent Retranslation Comparison: Kernelization for Independent-Set Reconfiguration

This record compares two translations of the statement layer for the independent-set reconfiguration kernel, covering Lemmas 6, 7, and 16; Construction 13/Proposition 14; Lemmas 18 and 4; and Theorem 1. It records how differences were adjudicated.

- Adjudication uses the original LIPIcs.STACS.2026.19 (Sections 3 and 4 of the component ledger).
- This is the second of three fidelity records (independent retranslation).

## Sources and independence

- **B version:** It used the full paper text (primarily the LIPIcs version, cross-checked against the longer arXiv version) and the list of results to translate plus definition-layer constraints (`SimpleGraph`, `ReflTransGen`, and branch sets). B did not see any Lean statements from A. Its transparency note says that after reading the paper and completing its analysis, B found and read the checkpoint sheet `checkpoints.md` in the same directory. The key judgments (the meaning of unlock, assembly of kernelConst, and the strengthened form of Lemma 6) had already been derived independently from the source. Thus, in the strict sense, B did not know A's Lean formulation but had seen A's plain-language checkpoint breakdown. The comparison focuses on whether B's independently identified premises, boundary cases, and reading choices appear unchanged in A.
- **A version:** Written earlier that day, before B was delivered, using the same paper text and the checkpoint sheet (`checkpoints.md`).
- The comparison focuses on quantifiers and premises, constants and thresholds, the readings of unlock and G′, and packaging of external facts.

## Findings from this comparison

There is no substantive disagreement. The versions independently reached the same conclusions on four key decisions: the main-theorem constant, the reading of unlock, the branch context for Lemmas 18/4, and packaging of external facts. B's list of 15 pitfalls was checked against A one by one: T1 (truncated subtraction) was checked case by case in A's checkpoint 6.5 record; T2 is deviation 5; T3 is the last row above; T4 (the q ≥ 1 pigeonhole argument) is covered by hq : 1 ≤ q in A's c4 ✓; T5 is the Lemma 4 row above; T6 is covered by A's c1 witness form; T7 is deviation 4; T8/T9 (reuse of k and |X| ≤ 2k) are stated correctly in A; T10 (a sequence of length zero) uses `ReflTransGen` in A ✓; T11 is in the row above; T12 (the subgraph-to-subgraph step uses the singleton branch set) matches A's proof path for c2; T13 (relaxed form of Corollary 10) uses the final relaxed bound in both; T14 (self-consistency at r = 1) was checked in A; T15 (no division issue when N3 = 0) is avoided because A's c2 statement contains no division ✓.

B's one more detailed finding does not affect the correctness of A's statement and is recorded here: §2.1 of the paper says to take χup := r + 2 for a general K_{3,r}-minor-free graph, which conflicts with Lemma 5 (Kostochka–Prince: (r+2)-degenerate ⟹ (r+3)-colorable) and the max{r,6300}+3 actually used in Lemma 11. This is an off-by-one error between degeneracy and number of colors (ambiguity A5). A consistently uses max{r,6300}+3, matching Lemma 11 and unaffected by the typo. B also found two minor errors in the long arXiv version (the class definition omits `\ X`, and “k-class” is misstated as |X| = k); A uses the LIPIcs conference version, so neither is relevant.

## Conclusion

The mathematical content of all seven statements agrees between the two translations. Each difference between the shipped A version and B (custom coloring predicate, predicate-style construction, combined c4, or removed hygiene premise) was adjudicated item by item and found not to change the statement. No disagreements remain unresolved.
