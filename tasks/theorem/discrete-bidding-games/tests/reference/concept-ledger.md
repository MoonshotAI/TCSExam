## 3. Deviation record

1. **Legal bids follow the convention of the model’s source [12], TheoretiCS 2025 §2.3:** only the player with the tie-breaking advantage may bid a starred value. If the holder has budget B*, every element of [0,B] in ℕ* is a legal bid; for the non-holder, the legal set is the ordinary integers {0,…,|B|}. This paper (CSL 2026) writes λ as “{0,…,B}”, which read literally as an interval in ℕ* includes starred bids for the player without the advantage. Under that literal reading, value iteration’s minimum does not match the actual game value (small example E9: 27 games × depth ≤ 3 × all configurations; zero mismatches with the restricted semantics, mismatches under both tie-breaking rules with the unrestricted interval). Corroboration: the response bids (0 or trump(B,b)) constructed for Cons in Appendix A are exactly legal under the restricted semantics; the only purpose of the star-matching correction in the second step of Definition 21 is to enforce this legality condition. The problem statement’s `PresLegal`/`ConsLegal`, `muN`, and `phi` all use `legalBids`.
2. **Correction to the truncation bound:** Equation (4) is printed with |V|·k·W, but the pigeonhole argument in Appendix D needs “number of configurations · W”; the number of configurations is |V|·(2k+2), since [k] has 2k+2 budget values. The printed bound fails at k = 0 (counterexample found by small example E6: v1→v0 with weight −2, v0 has a weight-0 self-loop, k = 0; the true energy threshold is 2 > 0 = |V|kW, so the printed bound would truncate it to ∞). This task sets cap = N·(2k+2)·W. Small example E10 verifies that, after this correction, “µ is finite iff the true threshold ≤ cap” holds on all configurations in all 27 games. The |V|kW+1 in Algorithm 1 pseudocode is replaced by the same correction.
3. **Definition of the fixed point µ:** the paper says “monotone + finite value range ⟹ reaches a fixed point.” This task defines `mu` as the maximum value in the truncated sequence within the a priori bound `stabSteps = N·(2k+2)·(cap+2)` (a finite maximum is attained); monotonicity and reaching a fixed point are checkpoint c3 obligations. The Bellman equation for µ (clause 3 of c3) is also required at µ = ∞, whereas the paper uses only finite coordinates (cross-checked in small example E10).
4. **Statement of the average property:** Definition 18 is printed as ⌊(|T(v⁺)|+|T(v⁻)|)/2⌋ + ε (floor; pdftotext incorrectly rendered it as ceiling, and the page image confirms the floor). The three values of ε—0 when the sum is even and T(v⁻) is ordinary; 1 when the sum is odd and T(v⁻) is starred; * otherwise—are equivalent to one equation: dbl(T(v)) = |T(v⁺)| + |T(v⁻)| + star(T(v⁻)). The four cases were checked individually and against the five identities in Observation 3. The task implements `avgVal` this way, with `AverageProperty T := ∀ v, T v = avgVal T v`. Note: in case (2), “T(v⁻) ∈ ℕ*” must mean “starred” (ℕ*  ℕ), according to Appendix F.
5. **Budget-agnostic predicate uses the Trim form:** the printed formal clause in Definition 20 requires only “same configuration, same bid,” but the prose in the same paragraph (“acts in B and B* as if the budget were Th(v) and Th(v)*”), Definition 26, and Remark 39 give the stronger Trim form: bids depend only on v and Trim(v,B). This task uses the stronger form (the constructed σagn satisfies it).
6. **Energy bound in Theorem 27:** the paper writes max_{v∈V} Energy(v, Th(v)); when Th(v) = k+1, Energy is ∞ and is outside the natural domain of the maximum. The task’s `maxThEnergy` counts such vertices as 0 (that is, it takes the maximum of µ(v, Th(v)) over vertices with Th(v) ≤ k*), matching the paper’s intent.
7. **Not packaged:** Theorem 15 (equivalence between energy and mean payoff; Appendix E includes liminf analysis) and the Cons dual in Section 6 (Theorem 34) are not packaged. The Section 7 NP∩coNP membership result is not a checkpoint under the usual convention (there is no complexity-class infrastructure). Lemma 25 and Claim 28 are proof details of c8, not separate checkpoints.
8. **Coordinate range for R_n energy in Appendix A:** printed as {−1,…,|V|·W}, but should be {−1,…,n·W} according to its use (µ_n ≤ nW can exceed |V|W). c2 is stated directly on the semantics of G_n and does not include R_n, so it is unaffected; recorded for reference.
9. **Appendix F labels elose formulas as ewin in several places** (for example, the b < cbid case on p. XX:29). The formula itself has the form of elose (maximum over neighbors, using B ⊕ trump); read according to its content. No statement is affected.
10. **Existential form of Lemma 11:** the paper names strategy σVI; c4 uses an existence statement (∃ legal strategy), with construction of σVI as proof content. c8 itself gives an explicit strategy form (∃ σ and budget agnostic).
11. **Degenerate case in Observation 3 (repaired after a defect was confirmed by the repository-wide falsification sweep):** Observation 3 in the paper (paper.txt:2156) is stated unconditionally but is false literally when T(v⁻) is starred and T(v⁺) = T(v⁻). The average property then forces T(v) = T(v⁻), and Definition 21’s bid b^T(v) = T(v) ⊖ (|T(v⁻)|+1) subtracts more than the minuend (0* ⊖ 1 is outside the effective domain on which ⊖ retains its exact definition; see the definition-layer notes for `Bdg.sub` and `dbl_sub`). Because `Bdg.sub` truncates, identities 1, 4, 5, and 7 of the seven identities fail. Counterexample in Section 3 of `group-4.md`: a one-vertex self-loop arena, k = 0, T ≡ 0*; all old premises hold, but the first clause reads 0 ≥ 0*. The same case can occur for the paper’s own Th (an arena with N = 2 and Th ≡ 0*). The defect is in the paper itself: ⊖ is used only when the result remains in ℕ*, but Observation 3 does not discuss this degenerate case. Repair: c6 adds premise `hnd : (G.nbrMin T v).star = true → G.nbrMin T v < G.nbrMax T v`, excluding exactly the degenerate case where nbrMin is starred and equals nbrMax. Verification: exhaustive numeric check at K = 10 (`verify/probe_c6_fix.py`, included in the verification pipeline)—the 11 excluded pairs are exactly all violating pairs; the seven identities have zero violations on retained cases; the retained region with starred nbrMin contains 110 pairs, so the repair is not vacuous. A Lean probe (`verify/probe_c6.lean`, also included) confirms at arena level that the counterexample satisfies every old premise but is excluded by hnd, and that on a legal instance the new premise and all seven conclusions hold. Use-site check: the paper cites Observation 3 only in the proof of Lemma 25 (paper.txt:2211, T = Th). At the degenerate vertex, hnd fails for Th, so the budget invariant cannot be applied there; Appendix H’s small-change argument in the proof of Claim 28 handles it separately. The only case where the trimmed budget does not match has a strictly increasing small-change value; c8’s proof idea already notes this, and its statement is unchanged.

## 4. Main-theorem assembly check (path to c8)

1. c3 gives the Bellman equation for µ and budget monotonicity; c4 uses the σVI invariant for the Pres direction; c5 uses negative-cycle restart for the Cons direction ⟹ Energy ≡ µ (content of Theorem 13).
2. `Th_spec` (fixed layer) characterizes Th(v): µ(v, Th(v)) < ∞ and µ(v, Th(v) ⊖ 0*) = ∞.
3. c7 (the eight-case analysis in Appendix F): µ(v, f(v)) < ∞ and µ(v, f(v) ⊖ 0*) = ∞, with f equal to the neighbor extremum’s average, implies f ≡ Th and hence the average property.
4. c6 gives the budget-invariant identities (take T = Th; c7 supplies its premise).
5. Lemma 25 (proof content of c8): for B ∈ {Th(v), Th(v)⊕0*}, the σVI bid equals bidTh(v,B), and the vertex lies in A^Th(v) (uses the ∞ direction of c5, so c8 includes `hdet`).
6. Define σagn(v,B) := σVI(v, Trim(v,B)); the small-change value ▽(i) = |B_i ⊖ Th(v_i)| is monotone and bounded, so it eventually stabilizes. Once stable, the energy invariant holds (Claim 28, proof content of c8); counting prefix segments (“patches”) gives M = (k+1)·(W + max_v Energy(v, Th(v))) (Appendix I).

## 5. SOP 6.5 error-pattern self-check record

1. **Constrained existential outputs:** c8’s ∃ σ has three constraints—legality, budget independence, and M-winning for every configuration with B ≥ Th(v). A constant strategy (bid 0 and choose a fixed neighbor) cannot satisfy the winning condition; the small-change/energy argument is its substantive content. The existential strategies in c4/c5 are similarly constrained by the exact value of M (for c5, M = µ−1 and the two ∀e branches).
2. **Degenerate parameters:** k = 0 (only budgets 0/0*), W = 0 (all edge weights zero), N = 1 (single-vertex self-loop), and B = 0 were checked by substitution. µ_n ≤ n·W gives µ ≡ 0 when W = 0, and the finite c5 branch is Cons (−1)-win (trivially at m = 0, matching the Appendix A base case ⟨v, −1, 0⟩). k = 0 is the key case for the cap correction (small example E6). Existential claims are not vacuous: at W = 0 all propositions can still hold (µ is all 0, Th ≡ 0, and both sides of the average property are 0).
3. **Main-theorem premises cover premises at each checkpoint call:** c8 invokes c6 at T = Th. hT (∀v, Th v ≤ k+1) follows from the definition of Th; havg is c7; hv (Th v ≤ k*) follows from the call-site conditions B ≥ Th(v) and B ≤ k*. On the degenerate vertex where Th(v⁺) = Th(v⁻) is starred, hnd added by the 2026-08-21 repair does not hold for Th, so the c8 proof does not call c6 in that case; Appendix H’s small-change argument handles it (see Deviation 11). c5 passes hB through to c2; c7 invokes c3 without an extra premise. ✓
4. **Substitution/evaluation consistency:** ewin uses B ⊖ b, elose uses B ⊕ trump(B,b), matching Equations (1)/(2) point for point (page image XX:7); the |T(v⁻)|+1 in Definition 21 and T(v⁻) ⊕ 0* in Definition 22 were checked against page images XX:10–11.
5. **Base case:** the induction base for c1/c2 is n = 0 (µ_0 ≡ 0), covered by the m ≤ 0 case in PresWinsTrunc/ConsWinsTrunc. ✓
6. **Both branches and off-by-one boundaries:** both c5 branches (∞ and finite) are present; c3 has both monotonicity directions, the fixed point, and Corollary 14. The five c6 identities were checked one by one against the page image for Observation 3 (XX:29), including the ⊕1 form (T⊕0*) ⊕ (bT⊕1) = (|T(v⁺)|+1)*.

The premise B ≥ Th(v) is empty when Th(v) = k+1 (there is no eligible configuration at that vertex), but the full statement is not vacuous for this reason—other vertices remain constrained.


## Statement requirements

- What this records: the repair of false statements in the main theorem c8, the fixed-point clause in c3, c4, and c7—mu changed from “the maximum of the a posteriori truncated sequence within the a priori bound” to “the value of the recursion-internally truncated operator iterate muC at stabSteps.”
- Cross-check: Equation (4) of the paper (arXiv 2509.00506v1), which truncates the sequence after iteration, and Algorithm 1 in Appendix B, which truncates inside the recursive operator iteration, are different sequences but have the same eventual fixed point. The paper says only “monotone + finite value range ⟹ reaches a fixed point” (the original Deviation 3 followed Equation (4), taking the maximum after truncation). Failure mechanism: the post-truncated sequence can continue to change after exceeding the cap, so its maximum within the a priori bound need not be a fixed point. The operator iteration places truncation inside the recursion (∞ is sticky) and is monotone increasing on the finite poset {0..cap}∪{⊤}; each coordinate can strictly increase at most cap+2 times, so it must stabilize by stabSteps = N·(2k+2)·(cap+2)+1, and mu is the true fixed point. There is no defect in the paper.
- Repair: drafted by astra and applied atomically across all three tiers by the acceptance side—after phi, add trim (values above cap become ∞), muC (recursively truncated operator iteration), and mu := muC stabSteps, plus three auxiliary theorems; remove the old mu and muTilde_le_mu_of_lt/mu_le_cap_or_eq_top/mu_eq_muTilde; retain muTilde as a derived concept but no longer use it to define mu. The checkpoint region (statements, sorry slots, and documentation) is byte-for-byte unchanged.
- One structural adjustment: moved the BEGIN-ONLY-GUIDED-LADDER marker from before subW to before the cast-coherence lemma group. After the repair, phi/subW/trim/muC/mu lie on the definition path to mu, and bare c8 depends on them through mu, so these definitions moved from the guided/ladder-only region to the region shared by all three tiers.
