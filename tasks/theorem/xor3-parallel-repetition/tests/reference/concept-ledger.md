## 3. Discrepancy register (all differences between the shipped statements and the source)

1. **Distribution representation:** The shipped version uses a mass function and explicit finite types; `μ^⊗n` acts on function-triple types (equivalent after transposition). The source's `t : supp(µ) → A` is represented as a total function (values outside the support do not affect any statement). Values use `Finset` maxima.
2. **Making c3 explicit:** The source has `val(G_µ^⊗n) ≤ val(G_µ′^⊗δn/2) + 2^{−Ω(δn)}`; the shipped statement is `val ≤ val(G_μ′^⊗⌊δn/2⌋) + exp(−δn/8)`. `exp(−δn/8)` is the explicit-constant version of the standard Chernoff lower-tail bound for `Binomial(n, δ)` at multiplicative deviation 1/2, exactly the bound used in the source proof (§3.3); `δn/2` is rounded down. D3 records two omissions in the source proof: the final tensor factor `⊗|I|` should be `⊗|[n]\I|`; and monotonicity of value in the repetition count is not proved. That monotonicity holds: winning one additional repetition cannot make the task easier, as is seen by fixing the final repetition.
3. **T in c4:** The source has `T : supp(µ) → D`; the shipped version uses a total function plus the closed-disk bound `‖T x y z‖ ≤ 1` on the support.
4. **Quantifier in c6:** “For all embeddings” in source Lemma 6.2 (with no bound on the group order; D6/D16) is represented as “for every embedding into a finite cyclic group `ZMod r` (`r ≥ 1`).” These are equivalent: the image of any embedding generates a finite abelian subgroup; if two points are distinguished by an embedding, a cyclic quotient (a character) of that subgroup distinguishes them. The converse is immediate. The master embedding is given by the existence of a finite direct product of cyclic groups in Definition 6.1 (the product itself is a finite abelian group). D8 records that the formal Definition 6.1 has no normalization condition, the overview in §2.1.3 does, and the two versions differ on a prime-power-order restriction; neither difference affects the existence statement.

5. **Packaging and explicit form of c7:** Lemma 8.1 (the paper's only result not proved there, obtained along the route in [BKM23c §2]) is packaged as the `FreimanAssumption` hypothesis. `Ω(ε^{O(p^k N)})|A|ⁿ` is made explicit as `∃ C ≥ 1, |A′| ≥ ε^{C·p^k·M}·|A|^n / C` (C absorbs both the multiplicative constant and the exponent constant; D10 records that the -layout text layer dropped k from the exponent, while the -raw layer confirms it is O(p^k N)). The printed Theorem 3 does not state that p is prime (D9); the shipped version adds the assumption `hp : p.Prime` (the proof uses reduction modulo p and “N a power of p”; primality is part of the standing setup in §6). The printed Theorem 3 omits the superscript n from the codomains of f,g,h (D9); based on context, the shipped version uses `(Fin n → A) → Fin n → ZMod (p^k)`.

   **Quantifier correction recorded 260918:** The initial shipped version placed `∃ C` inside `∀ n`, the strategy triple, and `∀ ε`, allowing C to be chosen separately for every instance. Taking `|A|ⁿ` makes the assumption unconditionally provable in Lean (with A′ = the singleton set {0}); when made explicit, the central meaning of the big-O constant being independent of n was lost. This was judged to be an error in the task statement, not a weakness in the paper's Lemma 8.1 (the standard reading is that the big-O constant does not depend on n, and the §8 descent argument uses this). This revision moves `∃ C` after the fixed data `(A, p, k, M)` but before `∀ n` (and the strategies and ε), while A′ remains existentially quantified per instance. Fidelity: the paper gives absolute constants c₀, C₀ such that `|A′| ≥ c₀·ε^{C₀·p^kN}·|A|ⁿ`. Set C = max(C₀, 1/c₀, 1); for every ε ≤ 1, `ε^{C·p^kN}/C ≤ c₀·ε^{C₀·p^kN}`, so the paper's form pointwise implies the revised form. The revised statement is therefore a weakened packaging of the paper's Lemma 8.1. Mechanism check (independent validation environment): the trivial witness C := |A|ⁿ is mechanically ruled out at elaboration time under the new quantifier order (`Unknown identifier 'n'`); an n-independent witness skeleton elaborates, the Freiman-homomorphism half closes for the singleton set, and the only remaining obligation is the size bound (the actual content of the paper's Lemma 8.1); the hypothesis domain is nonempty.
6. **Conditional form of c8:** The main theorem has `FreimanAssumption` as a hypothesis (the source's Theorem 1 is unconditional, but its complete proof relies on §8, whose Lemma 8.1 follows an external route). The source does not give the range of n (D1); the conventional condition `1 ≤ n` is added. The constant dependency is `c := c(G, µ)` (the main-text convention); D19 records that the abstract says c(G).
7. **Notation in the proof of c5** (affects one line of the source proof, not the statement): Read the source `1(S) = |{i : S_i = 1}|` literally as the number of coordinates equal to 1. Fix the coordinates where `Sᵢ ≠ 1` (the disk-valued factors contribute at most 1), apply Theorem 2 to the remaining coordinates (the resulting tensor is T itself), then average over S to obtain `(1 − (1−2^{−c})/m)ⁿ ≤ 2^{−c′n}`. If it is read as Hamming weight, it requires “T^s remains non-embeddable for s with gcd(s,m)>1”; D4/D15 give a counterexample to this strengthening (two-player simulation: µ is uniform on `{0,1}²`, t = x+2xy mod 4, and val = 3/4, but the operator for T² has singular value 1). Therefore the Hamming-weight reading is not used. The Lean statement for c5 is the source Corollary 4.1 and contains no proof notation.
8. **Signs in c1/c2:** F/G/H have positive signs and T_S has a negative sign in c1 (as in the source); c2 uses `T(x,y) = ω^{−t(x,y)}` with a minus sign (D5 records the source's inconsistent signs in two places: the statement in §2.1.1 has ω^{−t}, while a later expression uses ω^{+t}; conjugation invariance resolves this). The shipped version follows the statement, using `-t`. The absolute-value bars in the conclusion of c2 were lost in the text layer and confirmed from the page image (D2); the shipped version writes `‖·‖`.

## 4. Step-by-step check of the assembled main chain (compare the assumptions used to assemble c8)

1. **Reduction to prime-power cyclic groups** (opening of §6 and §2.1.1): The source gives this in two brief statements (fundamental theorem of finite abelian groups and the Chinese remainder theorem). Check: `Ans ≅ ∏ Z_{p_i^{k_i}}`; if a component game has a value-1 strategy, then the original game has a value-1 strategy by filling the other components with 0. Thus `val < 1` implies that every component game has value below 1. The winning event for the n-fold game requires winning in every component, so `val(G^⊗n) ≤ minᵢ val(Gᵢ^⊗n)`; taking the minimum of the component constants gives exponential decay for the original game. The c8 proof obligation includes this step (the guided-tier docstring states it).
2. **Non-embeddable branch** (c8 ← c5): `T = ω^t` is non-embeddable and has no Z-embedding ⟹ (Lemma 1.3) pairwise connected ⟹ c5. Lemma 1.3 is not a separate tested statement; its proof is part of the c8 proof obligation (a one-line construction in source §1.1), as noted in the guided-tier docstring.
3. **Embeddable branch** (c8 ← c6 + c7 + c2 + c3): If T is embeddable, then `|A|=|B|=|C|=1` on the support (the product has modulus 1) ⟹ the phase equation `a′+b′+c′ ≡ t/m (mod 1)` is a rational linear system, and a real solution implies a rational solution ⟹ there exist N and a,b,c such that `a+b+c ≡ N·t (mod p^k N)`; choose the least N. If N = 1, this is a value-1 strategy, contradicting `val < 1`; hence N > 1, and the hypothesis of c7 holds. The §6 reduction (the path argument in Lemma 6.5 preserves value; saturation in 6.6–6.8; merging in 6.9–6.10—the “merged game has value < 1” step uses two-player parallel repetition, i.e. c2/Raz98; the main relaxed base case in 6.12 uses the no-Z-embedding hypothesis via Dirichlet approximation; noise removal modulo the group and Theorem 5 in 6.13) transfers the strategy to the uniform zero-sum game on the main group, with winning probability `≥ ε^{O(1)}/2^{O(c′n)}` and n/8 coordinates. c7 supplies the final `2^{−c″n}` bound. This reduction is the main part of the c8 proof obligation (about 10 pages), as described in the guided-tier docstring. c6 is used in Lemma 6.13 at the step that makes the objective well-defined on fibers of the master embedding (Lemma 6.4).
4. **c5 ← c1 + c4:** See discrepancy 7.
5. **c4 ← c2 + c3 (inside §§4–5):** Path argument (4.6), merging (4.7–4.9, with 4.9 reduced to the two-player case, i.e. c2), and random restriction (c3) lead to the relaxed base case (4.10/4.11); Efron–Stein decomposition + effective noise (4.13, reduced to connected two-player games, i.e. c2) + SVD induction (§5) + Chebyshev gluing (4.12).
6. **c7 ← `FreimanAssumption`:** The descent chain in Lemmas 8.2–8.5 is part of the c7 proof obligation; the packaged hypothesis is the statement of Lemma 8.1 (made explicit as described in discrepancy 5).

Focused comparison of assumptions at use sites: The four c7 assumptions `1 ≤ k`, p prime, `1 < N`, and `IsMinLift` all hold at the instance where c8 invokes c7 (item 3 above). The pairwise-connectedness assumption of c5 follows from no Z-embedding via Lemma 1.3. The support-wise closed-disk bound for c4 holds for `T = ω^t` (`chi_norm`, proved in the fixed layer). The `val < 1` and connectedness assumptions for c2 at its §6.10 use site follow from the argument that “if the merged game had value 1, then the original game would have value 1” (the source cites Raz98; c2 is the analytic form for its XOR case).

## 5. Substitution of degenerate parameters

- **n = 0:** Both sides of c1 equal 1 (empty product/empty sum), so the equality holds ✓. The conclusions of c2/c4/c5/c7/c8 at n = 0 are `val ≤ 2^0 = 1`, which is always true; the shipped statements follow the source convention `1 ≤ n`, so this case does not apply.
- **The support cannot be empty:** `Dist`'s `sum_one` condition makes the support nonempty ✓; there is no empty-completeness failure mode like pcp-zero-check.
- **Singleton support:** If μ is a point mass, `NoZEmbedding` holds: `σ(x₀)+γ(y₀)+ϕ(z₀)=0` forces … Any embedding has one equation at the unique point, and σ, γ, ϕ each take only one value, so each is automatically constant ✓. The c8 premise `val < 1` for a singleton target t that cannot be represented as a sum gives val = 0; the conclusion `0 ≤ 2^{−cn}` is always true ✓ and is not vacuous (the GHZ game satisfies all premises and has a nontrivial conclusion; see below).
- **m = 1 (the trivial group `ZMod 1`):** In c1, S has only one value, so the equality holds directly ✓. The c5 premise `chi 1 ∘ t ≡ 1` is necessarily embeddable (A=B=C=1), so the premise of c5 is unsatisfiable and the statement is vacuously true, consistently with the source (Corollary 4.1 is already vacuously true for m = 1; this is not a weakening).
- **Singleton Σ in c6:** With no nontrivial embedding, the master embedding can be taken with s = 1 and q ≡ 1 (the trivial cyclic group); both sides of the capture property are always true ✓.
- **Trivial group A in c7:** The equation `x+y+z=0` has only `(0,0,0)`. `HasLift` holds for M=1 by taking a=b=c=0: `0 = 1·t(0,0,0)`; t has only one value and `1·t = t`. Hmm—if `t(0,0,0) ≠ 0`, M=1 does not work, but the premises `IsMinLift` and `1 < N` can still hold (take N equal to the order of p^k … with a ≡ t as a constant function … `a+b+c = N·t` mod p^kN: taking a = t(0,0,0)·1 … details: a constant a=t(0,0,0) gives a construction). The conclusion `val ≤ 2^{−cn}`: there is only one strategy, the “all-zero strategy”; if `t(0,0,0) ≠ 0`, its winning probability is 0 ✓; if it is 0, then M=1 has a lift, contradicting the minimality premise `1 < N`, so the premises cannot all hold ✓. No new vacuity is introduced.
- **Overall satisfiability check for the premises:** The GHZ game (μ uniform on the zero-sum subset of `Z_2³`, t = x∨y∨z mod 2) satisfies all c8 premises (`val = 3/4 < 1`; no Z-embedding, as solving the four linear equations directly shows that the three maps must each be constant), and its conclusion is the main result of [BKM23c], so the theorem is nontrivial ✓.
- **Weakening check** (6.5, item 7): Each shipped conclusion has the same form as the source (exponential `2^{−cn}`; explicit constants appear only in the error term in c3 and the packaged hypothesis in c7, both made explicit from bounds in the source proof). No premise is strengthened (the c7 assumption `hp : p.Prime` belongs to the standing setup of the source proof; see discrepancy 5).

## 7. Systematic check for common errors under SOP 6.5

1. **Constraints on existential witnesses:** The c6 output (the master-embedding triple) is tied to the original object by `IsEmbedding μ σm γm ϕm` (the zero-sum condition) plus the capture property (equivalence to the equality condition for all embeddings into finite cyclic groups). At the most degenerate choice (s = 0, the empty product/constant embedding), the right side of the capture property fails when a nonconstant distinguishing embedding exists; the left-side equality also fails, so the two directions do not collapse into a tautology ✓. The only output of c7 is a constant C, so there is no dimensional constraint issue. Same for c8.
2. **Degenerate parameters:** See the item-by-item substitutions in Section 5 (n = 0, m = 1, singleton support, trivial group A, and s = 1 trivial master embedding). Under these substitutions, each statement either holds or has an unsatisfiable premise; vacuity occurs only where it also occurs in the source, such as c5 for m = 1.
3. **Main theorem assumptions are at least as strong as the assumptions at each tested use site:** At the c8 use of c5, pairwise connectedness follows from no Z-embedding by Lemma 1.3, and the branch condition is non-embeddability ✓. At the use of c7, `1 ≤ k` (the §6 setup), p prime (the §6 setup), `1 < N` (from `val < 1` implying N ≠ 1, with existence supplied by embeddability), and `IsMinLift` (choose a minimum) ✓; each is checked in Section 4. The support-wise closed-disk bound for c4 holds at its c5 use site by `chi_norm` ✓.
4. **Substitution point and evaluation point agree:** The exponent of T_S in c1, `−Σᵢ Sᵢ·t(xᵢ,yᵢ,zᵢ)`, matches the source term by term (minus sign checked numerically via E9). c2's `T = ω^{−t}` follows the statement (D5). The zero-sum probability space for c7 is uniform on x+y+z=0 in `(Aⁿ)³`, as in §8. E8/E9 checked that its coordinate distribution equals the distribution of zero-sum triples: `Dist.pi3 (sumZeroDist A) n` is exactly a sequence of independent, identically distributed zero-sum triples coordinatewise, i.e. uniform zero-sum triples in `(Aⁿ)³` as in the source.
5. **Base conditions:** There are no induction or recursion statements (the induction in §5 is inside the c4 proof obligation). c3 includes `1 ≤ n` and `0 < δ ≤ 1`; c7 includes `1 ≤ k` and `1 < N`.
6. **Two-sided bounds and off-by-one issues:** The equality in `NonembeddableD` holds throughout the support (a pointwise universal condition, with no one-sided truncation). The mixture in c3 is the pointwise equality `μ = δμ′ + (1−δ)ν` (two-sided control); note that this is somewhat stronger than the source's pointwise condition `αµ′ ≤ µ` as used in §3.3, but agrees with the statement of Lemma 2.3, `µ = δµ′ + (1−δ)ν`. The bounds `‖T‖ ≤ 1` in c4 and `‖F‖ ≤ 1` in c2/c4 are closed-disk (two-sided modulus) bounds.
7. **Weakening and vacuity:** Each conclusion has the same form as the source (the explicit forms are itemized in Section 3); satisfiability of the premises is checked by the GHZ game (end of Section 5). The packaged assumption for c7 states the paper's Lemma 8.1 directly and is not strengthened (its quantifier scope `∀ A, p, k, M, n, ε` matches its use in the source).
